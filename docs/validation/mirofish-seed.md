# Licits launch in Romania: who adopts it, who resists, what happens

## The product
Licits (licits.com) is an invite-only beta tool for Romanian public procurement. It pulls open tenders from SEAP/SICAP several times a day, ranks them against a contractor's profile, checks eligibility requirement by requirement (met / not met / unclear), and drafts the technical offer and the DUAE. It never submits anything; the company always decides. Plans per month excluding VAT: Start 199 RON, Growth 349 RON, Pro 499 RON. Two free weeks for new users. Interface in English and Romanian.

## Market facts (measured on real SEAP data, 30 days to 28 Oct 2026)
- 771 contract awards in the covered sectors, 538 different winning companies.
- Construction works (CPV 45): 176 awards with a named winner, 137 different winners, only 24 of them won more than once in the month.
- Median construction award about 35 million RON; the lower quartile about 12 million RON; 14 percent of awards are under 5 million RON.
- Median number of offers received is 3; 23 percent of construction awards had a single bidder.
- About 487 to 551 new construction tenders are published each month.

## Back-test of the matching engine
Using only a company's other wins to build its profile, the tender it actually won ranked in the top 5 percent of about 3,700 open tenders in 2 of 3 cases (27 cases, 16 companies). In one third of cases the engine gave zero because the company bid on a different CPV code than before.

## People and organisations in this market
- Mihai Popescu, owner of a 12-person electrical and solar installation firm in Cluj. Wins 3 to 5 small public tenders a year, prepares every offer himself at night, reads Romanian only, distrusts software that "decides for him".
- Elena Ionescu, accountant for a 40-person road and utilities contractor in Bucharest. Assembles the DUAE and certificates by hand, worried about missing a deadline, controls the software budget.
- Radu Stanciu, owner of a mid-size building contractor in Timisoara with 90 staff. Already wins tenders above 20 million RON, employs one full-time tender specialist, hesitant to change process.
- Andreea Dumitru, independent public procurement consultant in Iasi. Prepares offers for 8 small clients, sees Licits as either a tool that multiplies her output or a threat that removes her fee.
- Constantin Barbu, owner of a family road-works company in Oltenia, 25 staff, does not use e-mail for tenders, hears about tools from his accountant and his competitors.
- ConsultTender SRL, a procurement consultancy that charges 1,500 to 4,000 RON per offer prepared.
- Manual SEAP monitoring, the default alternative: a person checks the portal each morning.
- Existing tender alert websites and newsletters that list notices but do not check eligibility.
- SEAP/SICAP, the state portal, slow and Romanian-only, the source of all tender data.
- ANAF, the tax authority, whose public registers supply company and turnover data to Licits.
- Contracting authorities such as city halls and county councils, which publish the tenders and care about receiving valid, complete offers.
- Camera de Comert si Industrie Cluj, a local business association that runs member events and recommends tools to members.
- A Romanian construction trade forum where owners compare tools and warn each other about bad experiences.
- Ioana Marinescu, owner of a 6-person HVAC and plumbing firm in Brasov. Bids on school and clinic renovations, has never used a paid tool, asks other owners on the forum before spending any money.
- Vasile Toma, owner of a 30-person water and sewage works company in Galati. Wins with low prices, worried that tools make everyone bid the same, suspicious of subscriptions.
- Gabriela Savu, office manager of a 15-person roofing company in Sibiu. Does all the paperwork, would adopt the DUAE drafting immediately, needs the owner's approval to pay.
- Florin Dragomir, owner of a young 8-person solar installation start-up in Constanta, founded 2022, has few references, afraid that experience requirements shut him out.
- Cristina Lazar, procurement lawyer in Cluj. Reviews offers for legal mistakes, will criticise any tool that generates legal documents and warns clients about liability.
- Bogdan Preda, tender specialist at a large construction group in Bucharest. Uses an internal spreadsheet system, sees Licits as a toy for small firms but watches for features.
- Simona Radu, bid manager at a 60-person electrical contractor in Craiova. Spends 15 hours per tender, would use eligibility checks but wants proof of accuracy first.
- Dan Iliescu, owner of a 20-person road marking and signage firm in Pitesti. Wins many small contracts below 1 million RON, price-sensitive, counts every monthly fee.
- Monica Craciun, accountant who runs the books for eleven small contractors in Oradea. Recommends tools to all of them, would earn trust or lose it for every client at once.
- Adrian Nistor, owner of a 45-person civil works firm in Suceava, wins mostly county council tenders, relationship-driven, thinks tools cannot replace knowing the buyer.
- Liviu Ene, former civil servant now freelance tender consultant in Ploiesti. Knows SEAP deeply, would test Licits by looking for mistakes and tell people publicly.
- Teodora Matei, advisor at a regional development agency in Iasi who helps small firms access EU and public funding, would recommend a trusted tool in workshops.
- Razvan Oprea, founder of a Romanian construction-tech newsletter with 8,000 subscribers, writes honest reviews and a launch mention could bring dozens of sign-ups.
- Alina Vasile, journalist at a business news site who covers public procurement scandals, interested in anything that increases transparency or reveals bid patterns.
- Ovidiu Mocanu, mayor of a mid-size town in Mures county, whose city hall publishes 40 tenders a year and receives too few offers on small jobs.
- Primaria Municipiului Bacau, a city hall publishing mostly road and building tenders, often receives only one offer.
- Consiliul Judetean Dolj, a county council with large infrastructure tenders and a procurement office of five people.

- Asociatia Romana a Antreprenorilor de Constructii (ARACO), the construction contractors' association with regional chapters.
